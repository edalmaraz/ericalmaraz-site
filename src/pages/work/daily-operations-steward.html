Five systems, case study 5 of 5, automation and AI

One brief each morning that says what needs me.

Running several brands across a support desk, shipping, inventory, email and a calendar, I had no single place that said what needed attention today. I built a steward that gathers it and tells me. In seven weeks it sent 36 morning briefs and logged 1,308 events.

  • My roleI asked for it and decide what each alert should do. AI agents wrote the code.
  • In serviceSince July 2026, rebuilt and extended that September
  • SettingOne operations lead, several brands, many tools

The situation

The information I needed each morning existed, but it lived in a support desk, a shipping platform, an inventory system, an inbox and a calendar. Checking each one by hand took time, and anything I did not check stayed unseen.

I wanted the reverse: the systems report to me, in one message, ranked by what needs me first.

My part, and the agents' part

I asked for it, starting with the morning brief, and I decide what each kind of alert should do. One ruling of mine: an item that is merely due soon stays silent, so the brief does not turn into noise.

AI agents wrote the code, first as a 13-task plan in July 2026 and then in a larger build-out that September. Its summaries are written by AI jobs, each with its own written instructions for the topic, sent through the gateway.

The result

  • 36 briefssent in the morning, and 28 end-of-day wraps
  • 1,308events logged in seven weeks, from 29 September to 16 November 2026
  • 34 situationsopened for me to act on, and 53 escalations raised

How this was counted. From the steward's own event log on 16 November 2026.

Not measured. Time saved each morning, and how many problems were caught earlier because of a brief.

Limits and what is next

The log shows 34 situations opened and only 2 closed. Either I am closing them somewhere the log does not see, or open items are piling up. I need to find out which before I claim it keeps my list clean.

It is built for one user, me. Two of its own automated checks are currently reported as failing, and fixing those comes before adding anything new.

Built with Python, SQLite, Windows Task Scheduler, a phone messaging bot, headless Claude jobs and a local model for summaries.
