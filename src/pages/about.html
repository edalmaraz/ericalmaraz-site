What teaching gave me

A classroom is an operation. A room full of people has to get through the same process at different speeds, and you find out quickly whether your instructions were clear.

That is where I learned to write a procedure someone else can follow, and to explain a system in plain words. Both turned out to be most of the job in operations, and nearly all of the job when the one following the procedure is an AI agent.

How the tools get built

  1. I write the specification. What the tool is for, what it reads, what it produces, and how I will know it is finished.
  2. I set the rules it must obey. For example: read-only access to the systems that hold orders and money, and no message ever sent to a customer.
  3. AI agents write the code. They also write the tests, under those rules.
  4. Checks decide, not opinions. Work is accepted when tests and checks I defined pass, and I verify the result against real records before I trust it.

I am not a software engineer by title. I own the design, the rules and the decision about when the work is good enough.

What I will and will not claim

Most of these tools have one user, and it is me. They are how I keep watch over orders and support for several brands at once. Where a page gives a number, it says how the number was counted. Where I have not measured something, such as hours saved, the page says that too.

Each case study ends with its limits, because a system I cannot criticize is a system I do not understand yet.

Why there are no names here
